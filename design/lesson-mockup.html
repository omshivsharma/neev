The idea

Return on your returns

📖 3 min read · reference for later

Socho aap ek ₹200 Swiggy order jitna paisa kahin invest karte ho, aur har saal thoda return milta hai. First year, you earn on your ₹200. The quiet trick is the second year: you earn a return on the original ₹200 and on last year's earnings too. Paisa khud paisa banane lagta hai.

For a while it feels boring — the early years look almost flat and you'll think "kuch ho hi nahi raha." That's normal. Compounding is back-loaded: most of the magic shows up in the later years, once the snowball is big enough to matter.

Two things decide how strong it gets: how long the money stays invested, and whether you resist pulling it out early. Every withdrawal resets the snowball to the bottom of the hill.

So "kitna return milega" is the wrong first question. The better ones — how long can I leave this untouched, and can I avoid touching it — are the parts you actually control. We won't name a single fund or stock; compounding is just a force, and where you point it is a separate call for later.

Two terms, plainly
Compounding
Earning returns on both your original money and the returns it has already earned.
Time horizon
How long you plan to leave money invested before you actually need it.

How tax touches this
Frequent buying & selling can trigger taxes and charges that interrupt compounding. In India, equity held over 1 year (long-term) is taxed differently from under 1 year (short-term). (verify current rate before relying on this)
